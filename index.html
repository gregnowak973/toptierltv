Three things every operator tells us

Different words, the same three problems — and each one has a data answer.

01

“We want more leads.”

You don’t need more leads. You need the slice of your territory with a fourteen-year-old furnace. A permit is a dated property event; we turn your service area into a list of households by what’s in the basement and how old it is.

A 2011 permit puts this household inside its replacement window today. Nobody had to guess.illustrative

02

“Our leads stop at ten miles.”

They don’t stop; they stop paying for the drive. A repair call past the radius loses money on truck-hours. A replacement doesn’t. We target by drive time and by job, so the far edge of your territory only gets the work that pays for the trip.

Rings by drive time, not a circle on a map. The far ring still has households worth a truck — if the job is a replacement.illustrative

From every household in the window to the ones that actually get mail: your own customers go to your CSRs, the unreachable drop out, and the holdout is set aside before anything prints.illustrative

03

“We don’t know if our marketing works.”

Because nothing was held back to compare against. Before the first piece mails, a slice of your households is set aside and signed off by you. At day 90 the answer is one number: booked jobs per thousand households, targeted versus held out.

The holdout gets everything your marketing normally does — except our mail. Whatever separates the two columns at day 90 is the program, not the season.illustrative

How it works

Three phases. The first is free, and each one earns the next.

01

Your territory, scored Free

A task-by-task table for your service area from public records: how many households are entering the replacement window for each thing you sell, where they cluster by drive time, and — honestly — which tasks the record can’t see well enough to target. Every number carries its evidence grade. No data from you required.

Replacements cluster between 12 and 18 years of equipment age. The score is how many of your households are in that band right now.illustrative

02

The campaign 90 days

Households, not zip codes. We build the list at the parcel level, keep your existing customers for your own team, and reach the rest at home — by mail, on the big screen, online — with creative that knows what’s in their basement. Your holdout is set aside before the first send. Media runs at cost; you see every invoice.

The same list, a different piece for each house: the creative names the equipment the permit named. Nobody gets a generic mailer.illustrative

03

The readout Day 90

Booked jobs per thousand targeted households, targeted versus held out, with the confidence interval and the cost per booked job. Cross-checked against markets we chose before the campaign. If it didn’t work, the readout says so. That’s the product.

The shape of the day-90 answer: two bars, the gap between them, and the interval around the gap. The heights here are drawn, not measured — yours come from your own bookings.illustrative

What it is not

Not a lead vendor. Not a retainer for awareness. Not a software project.

You never pay per lead

Nobody else gets your list. Every dollar goes to a household we can name, and every household we name is one we can measure.

You keep your stack

No integration. The territory table needs nothing from you; the campaign needs a suppression list; the customer-side plays, when you want them, need one scheduled export from your field-service software.

Your agency mails a zip code. We mail the households in it that are due — and we can tell you which ones booked.
